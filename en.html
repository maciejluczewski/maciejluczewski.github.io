THE L3 TRILOGY

a story that begins with being watched — and ends with passing it on

Three volumes. One story told in three gestures: first we discover that someone is watching us. Then we decide to answer — not with words, but with twenty years of work and a ship that should not exist. In the end we understand that we are a link in a chain — and we pass it on.

This is hard SF with one iron rule: the physics has to hold, and greatness is measured in decisions, not explosions. And beneath the story — a complete, original theory of the universe, free to download below. Because questions are not for sale.

The novels are currently published in Polish. The theory and the propulsion study below are fully available in English.

VOLUME I

L3: THE TROJAN OBSERVER

How long were we not alone before we noticed?

It begins innocently: an astrophysicist notices that the orbits of a few objects in the Solar System are a hair too perfect. A hair is not enough for an alarm. It is enough for insomnia.

This is not an invasion story. Nobody shoots at saucers here. It is worse: a growing, cold certainty that something has been watching us for a very long time. And then come three December nights after which the world wakes up smaller by six and a half billion people — and has to decide who it will be next.

A discovery thriller written to the rhythm of data, night phone calls and decisions that cannot be undone. After the last page you will look at the night sky differently. Not with fear. With vigilance.

“On one side of the line was the world he had served for twenty-eight years. On the other side — everything else.”

VOLUME II

L3: HORIZON

What can you build from nothing, if you remember what for?

Do you know the hardest thing about the end of the world? That the next day is another day.

Cinema can film a catastrophe. It cannot film the twenty years that come afterwards — and this book is about exactly those years. About people who, in a world without a grid and without illusions, decide not to rebuild the old world, but to build an answer. Because up there, someone is waiting for one.

Five Ignitions, five acts of stubbornness: a power plant on the Baltic coast that wakes first. “Made, not found.” Cold fire. And a ship built by a generation that knows it will not fly on it.

If you love “The Martian” for its faith in engineering and Liu Cixin for the breath of centuries — this is your shelf. Hope is not a feeling. Hope is a craft.

“The power comes from a real grid — from a heat-and-power plant brought back to life, block by block.”

VOLUME III

L3: THE GARDENERS

What do we pass on, when we pass on everything?

Do you know how trilogies about first contact end? With a great battle or a great revelation. This one ends with a conversation — and it is the most breathtaking ending you will get in years.

A ship built by the hands of survivors carries a handful of people toward something that has been waiting longer than our species has existed. There are children born between the stars, for whom Earth is a bedtime story. There are threads — dreams spun at night between the children of the ship and their peers back home. And there are the Gardeners. We will not tell you who they are.

At the end waits something unprecedented: a complete scientific treatise, bound like a relic. You can skip it. You will not.

You are not alone. This time it is us saying it.

“We carried the water. We drank. Now we carry it on — the well has no bottom, it has another shore.”

CTCUI — the theory the trilogy is built around

Cosmic Theory of Consciousness and Universe Inheritance · free to download, in full

What if black holes are not graves, but wombs? What if our universe is one leaf on a growing tree of cosmoses — and the accelerating expansion that astronomers patch with “dark energy” is simply the pressure of newborn children upon the parent?

CTCUI is an original cosmological hypothesis that brings several of the greatest puzzles of modern physics into a single frame: the Hubble tension, the nature of dark energy, the black hole information paradox, the fine-tuning of fundamental constants — and the hard problem of consciousness. Its core: universes form an evolving population; consciousness is quantum information that does not perish with the death of the body, but is inherited. The theory makes seven groups of testable predictions — and honestly states what would refute it.

This is not a revelation and not a dogma. It is a hypothesis put forward with honesty and seriousness. If you have read “The Gardeners” — this is that treatise. If you have not — open it on any page.

How would a ship built by survivors get there?

QFHPS — a hybrid interstellar propulsion concept · technical study, free to download

The ship from “HORIZON” does not have an engine out of a hat. It has a concept assembled from elements physics knows — combined into a whole it cannot yet build: plasma ignition, a scoop that collects fuel along the way instead of carrying it, the catalytic hot CNO cycle (which is why they had to mine Callisto before departure), a magnetic sail brake that sheds speed against the interstellar medium — without a gram of propellant — and Casimir cavities as the only negative energy ever measured in a laboratory.

The study is honest with the physics: you will find the calculations that hurt (how long acceleration really takes at a fraction of g; what a milligram of dust does at eight percent of the speed of light), and an explicit status for every claim — fact, extrapolation, or clearly marked speculation.

For the hard-SF reader: dessert. For the engineer and the student: an invitation to calculate better. For whoever one day builds such a drive: a seed.

ABOUT THE AUTHOR

Maciej Łuczewski is a Polish writer and independent researcher fascinated by the intersection of science, history and philosophy. He is the author of the hard science fiction trilogy “L3” (“The Trojan Observer”, “HORIZON”, “THE GARDENERS”) and the creator of the Cosmic Theory of Consciousness and Universe Inheritance (CTCUI), which explores the idea that black holes may be gateways to new universes and consciousness a fundamental part of the cosmos.

His books combine scientific precision with poetic imagination. He writes in Polish and lives in Gdynia — the city that, in his novels, builds a starship.
